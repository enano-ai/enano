## Sunday, 19 October

The envelope is still on the fridge. It has been there since the beginning of February, which means it has been there for eight months, which means the memory clinic letter inside it has been sitting in my kitchen for longer than it took me to grow a whole human being. Every time I open the fridge for milk I see it and I think: power of attorney. And then I close the fridge and make tea and think about something else, because thinking about it properly means ringing the solicitor back and admitting I still haven't found Mum's birth certificate, and that is a forty-minute phone call I do not have in me on a Tuesday.

I keep a list in my head of the things I am definitely going to do, and it is a long list, and none of it has dates on it. The 10k is in May. I have known about the 10k since last November. I have not entered it, because entering it would make it real, and I have run twice since August — once around the block with Theo on his scooter, which mostly involved stopping.

Dan said the thing about the will again on Thursday, in the kitchen, in the tone he uses when he has decided to be reasonable about something. He is right, obviously. We are renting, we have two children and a mortgage's worth of savings that doesn't exist, and my mother is disappearing by inches. Of course we should have a will. But the same part of my brain that won't ring the solicitor about the power of attorney also won't book an appointment about a will, and I notice that the two things are actually the same thing, which is: I do not want to sit in a room and describe what happens if I die.

The savings account has £2,300 in it. It has had £2,300 in it since June. The plan was £6,000 by the end of the year and I think, honestly, I knew when I wrote that down that it wasn't going to happen — the bank shifts dropped when the ward got quieter and then the car needed doing and then it was summer. I have been telling myself I'll pick it up in January. January is a very useful place to put things.

The course is the other one. Product management. I have been saying it for two years now — that I'll do a course, that I'll move out of the ward and into something where the ceiling isn't made of shift patterns. I have read the same three course pages maybe fifteen times. I have never once compared them to each other or written down what I would actually do with the thing at the end.

And then there is the ordinary business of being a person, which is mostly: the phone in bed again, the doomscrolling, the notification on Sunday telling me my screen time is up 14%. The bottle of red on the counter that gets opened on a Wednesday now, not a Friday. The swimming lessons I meant to book in September. The stack of books in the passenger seat of the car, which I move to the back seat when someone gets in, and which I have not opened once.
